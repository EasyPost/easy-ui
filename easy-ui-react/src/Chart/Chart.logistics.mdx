# Logistics intelligence

Six additive recipes cover carrier decisions and parcel tracking. All records are synthetic, use explicit observation windows and units, and drive both the plotted marks and exact-value tables. The existing eighteen analytical examples and six native components remain available.

## Delivery promises

<Canvas of={Stories.TransitReliability} />

Use a fully aged acceptance cohort for the fixed-cohort cumulative delivery curve. Keep unresolved parcels in its denominator; filtering to delivered parcels overstates reliability. This example uses calendar days and a 3-day promise with a 90% service target. It is not a survival estimator. Recent cohorts need an explicit censoring method and a documented business-calendar definition before making carrier comparisons.

## Rate competitiveness

<Canvas of={Stories.RateCompetitiveness} />

Center a diverging scale on price parity. Negative values are cheaper than the matched benchmark; positive values are more expensive. Match service, zone, billable weight, surcharges and effective dates. Suppress under-covered cells rather than filling them with zero. Exact offered/benchmark rates, matched observations, and opportunity volume accompany each cell.

## Pricing decisions

<Canvas of={Stories.PriceVolumeResponse} />

The supplied scenarios show volume and variable contribution at candidate prices, with a proposed-price marker. The shaded low/high volume envelope is a scenario range, not a statistical confidence interval. The contribution calculation assumes $4.60 variable cost per parcel, before fixed costs. Production callers own demand estimation, elasticity, fee treatment and validation; the curve does not establish causal lift from a price change.

## Capacity

<Canvas of={Bullets.WarehouseCapacity} />
<Canvas of={Bullets.ForecastCapacity} />
<Canvas of={Stories.CapacityPlanning} />

Use native bullets for an observed measure or a forecast peak against the same capacity scale. Use the full trend to see when overload begins. The example distinguishes solid observed volume from dashed forecasts and shows an 80% advisory band and a hard operating limit. Capacity must describe the same facility, service, time bucket and unit as the volume.

## Single-parcel event progress

<Canvas of={Stories.ShipmentTimeline} />

Observed scan endpoints bound dwell/movement intervals. Missing scans remain an explicit unknown segment. The ongoing final leg stops at the observation cutoff; a separate predicted arrival interval and promise marker do not imply a delivered event. Exact UTC timestamps and evidence labels are available in the table. Elapsed time is not percent complete.

## Multiple warehouses and parcels

<Canvas of={Stories.WarehouseParcelProgress} />

Each parcel uses one shared UTC axis. Solid spans end at the last observed event. A stale scan leaves an unknown gap before its forecast window; delivered parcels have no forecast bar. The table carries parcel ID, origin warehouse, status, last scan location/time and arrival bounds. The chart shows event progress rather than an invented continuous position.

Geographic mapping is developed separately from the chart portfolio. Event timelines remain available here; they share identifiers and timestamps with the mapping work.
